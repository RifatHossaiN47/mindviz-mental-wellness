COGNITIVE REFRAMING

OVERVIEW:
Cognitive reframing is a psychological technique that consists of identifying and then changing the way situations, experiences, events, ideas, and emotions are viewed. It's a core component of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT).

HOW IT WORKS:
Our thoughts influence our feelings and behaviors. By changing negative thought patterns, we can change how we feel and respond to situations.

INSTRUCTIONS:
1. Identify the negative thought
   Example: "I'm going to fail this exam"

2. Challenge the thought
   Ask yourself:
   - Is this thought based on facts or feelings?
   - What evidence supports or contradicts this thought?
   - Am I jumping to conclusions?
   - What would I tell a friend in this situation?

3. Generate alternative thoughts
   Example alternatives:
   - "I've studied and prepared as much as I could"
   - "Even if I don't do perfectly, I'll learn from the experience"
   - "One exam doesn't define my worth or future"

4. Choose a balanced thought
   Example: "I've prepared well and will do my best. The outcome doesn't define me."

5. Practice the new thought
   Repeat it whenever the negative thought appears

COMMON THINKING ERRORS:
- All-or-nothing thinking
- Overgeneralization
- Mental filtering (focusing only on negatives)
- Catastrophizing
- Personalization
- Should statements

BENEFITS:
- Reduces negative emotions
- Improves mood
- Increases resilience
- Better problem-solving
- Enhanced self-esteem

BEST FOR:
- Negative thinking patterns
- Low mood
- Anxiety about future events
- Self-criticism

DURATION: 8-15 minutes per session
DIFFICULTY: Moderate
EVIDENCE: Extensively validated in clinical research, recommended by WHO
